Traceability & Provenance

Sustainability Is
Not a Label.

It's a practice.

While not producing any clothes would be the most sustainable thing, that narrative fails to recognize the importance the textile industry holds in serving communities, sustaining expression of art, preserving craft & culture.

Our view on sustainability is producing garments with the least amount of environmental, social, or animal impact — and the most amount of longevity, reusability, & recyclability. We believe in achieving that through fundamental transparency — about the fabrics we use, why we use or don't use them, about where we source from, & about how we produce.

70%

of fashion's environmental footprint comes from the choice of materials alone

Because of this, we spent two years finding ways to establish a responsible supply chain — one that is short, sustainable, and intentional. Every decision has been guided by a value system of three pillars: the art, the craft, and the impact.

Provenance Journey

Every Garment
Has a Story

Each of our garments comes with a QR code that transparently shares the known history and journey of that piece. From farm, to fiber, to yarn, to fabric, to finishing & dyeing, and eventually — garment making.

1

Farm

Where our raw materials are responsibly sourced — from regenerative wool farms in Uruguay to organic cotton fields in India.

2

Fiber

Natural and cellulosic fibers are processed using methods that minimize water, chemical use, and environmental harm.

3

Yarn & Fabric

Spun and woven by partner mills — from Italian houses pioneering sustainable wool to Indian handlooms with multi-generational ties to weavers.

4

Finishing & Dyeing

Finished with care using low-impact dyes and processes that respect both artisans and the environment.

5

Garment Making

Handcrafted by multi-generational artisan families in India, whose techniques — Aari embroidery, Chikankari, hand-weaving — span centuries.

Our Materials

What We Use

We hold ourselves to the highest standards when selecting materials. Where we were unable to source materials meeting our strict criteria for comfort, quality, and environmental and animal welfare — we created entirely new supply chains.

Natural Fibers

Renewable & Biodegradable

Organic Cotton

Grown without synthetic pesticides or fertilizers, supporting soil health and farmer communities while minimizing chemical runoff.

Organic Wool

Sourced from farms that uphold high standards of animal welfare and land stewardship, with a focus on regenerative farming practices.

Peace Silk

An ethical alternative to conventional silk — the silkworm completes its life cycle naturally before the cocoon is harvested, ensuring no harm in the process.

Cellulosic Fibers

Plant-Based & Low Impact

Lyocell

Made from sustainably sourced wood via a closed-loop process that recycles over 99% of water and solvents. Exceptionally soft with a fraction of the environmental footprint.

Banana Fiber

Extracted from the stems of banana plants — agricultural byproducts usually discarded or burned — reducing crop-burning pollution and providing additional income for farming communities.

Lotus Fabric

One of the rarest textiles in the world, derived from lotus plant stems. Naturally breathable, lightweight, and produced with minimal environmental impact.

NATIVA™ Certified Wool

Blockchain-Verified

Regenerative Merino

One of our defining materials — sourced from farms in Uruguay that uphold high standards of animal welfare, land stewardship, and full blockchain-backed traceability from farm to finished garment.

Deadstock Fabrics

We repurpose surplus fabrics from luxury mills that would otherwise go to waste — extending their lifecycle and reducing demand for new production. These are the exception to our full traceability, as their prior journey is not always documented.

"Building a tangible, traceable journey for each of our fabric platforms has been extremely challenging but deeply rewarding."

— Tamanna Mullen, Founder

Our Standards

What We Don't Use

There are materials whose environmental, ethical, or recyclability costs we believe are simply too high. Here's what we've excluded from our supply chain — and why.

Leather & Fur

There is nothing ethical about clothing made from animals' skin. The production of leather and fur contributes to climate change, land devastation, pollution, and water contamination.

Virgin Polyester & Nylon

Derived from non-renewable petroleum resources through energy-intensive production processes. These synthetic fabrics are non-biodegradable and contribute to microplastic pollution with every wash.

Poly-Cotton Blends

While common in the industry, poly-cotton blends are extremely challenging to recycle on a large scale. The inseparable fusion of natural and synthetic fibers creates end-of-life complications that undermine circularity.
